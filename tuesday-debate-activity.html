⚔️ Challenge Prompts — Use These During the Debate!

Challenge #1

"If your organelle is so important — what would actually happen to the cell WITHOUT it? Be specific."

Tip: The best answers use specific consequences, not just "the cell would die."
Challenge #2

"My organelle does everything yours does, AND more. Why should we pick yours?"

Tip: Defend your organelle's unique job — what CAN'T another organelle replace?
Challenge #3

"Your organelle is only important in ONE type of cell. Mine works in ALL cells. Doesn't that make mine more essential?"

Tip: Think about whether your organelle is in plant cells, animal cells, or both.
Challenge #4

"My organelle is the reason YOU have energy right now. Can yours say that?"

Tip: Connect to cellular respiration, ATP, or the role in energy production.

Part 3 — What's Next: Cell Division (15 min)

🔁 From What We Know → What's Next

You know: Cells have organelles that work together to keep the cell alive.
You know: Cells need energy (ATP) to do all their jobs.
Big question coming up: Where do NEW cells come from? Remember Cell Theory — "new cells come from existing cells." But HOW exactly?
Next topic: Cell Division — specifically mitosis — how one cell copies itself to make two identical cells.

🤔 Think About This…

When you practice volleyball, your muscles get stronger. Do you know why? When you work a muscle hard, some muscle cells actually break down — and your body builds NEW ones to replace them (through cell division). Healing a scrape, growing taller, replacing skin cells — it all comes down to one thing: cells copying themselves.

And here's the wild part: every single organelle inside the cell has to be copied PERFECTLY before the cell can divide. Your nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes — all of it doubles first.
