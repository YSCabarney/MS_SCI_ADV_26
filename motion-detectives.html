Motion Investigation Bureau — Opening Case

Motion Detectives

A 15-minute warm-up mission. You will NOT be given any science vocabulary today — your job is to notice patterns in how three "Movers" travel, and give those patterns your own nicknames. The real vocabulary gets revealed at the very end.

⏱ 15 minutes 👥 6 detectives · 3 breakout rooms 🎯 No vocab needed yet

Mission Briefing

The case: Three "Movers" were caught on the record — an elevator, a sprinting animal, and a runner on a loop track. Headquarters recorded exactly where each Mover was located every few seconds, but nobody has explained why they moved the way they did. That's where you come in.

Each breakout room gets ONE Mover and TWO detective jobs. You and your partner each hold half the evidence — you'll need to talk to each other to crack the case. Nobody in your room has the same job, and no room has the same Mover.

When we're back together, each room reports its findings in under 90 seconds. Then Headquarters (your teacher) reveals the real scientific names for what you discovered — in your Motion Files notes.

ROOM 1

Mover A — "The Elevator"

Evidence: position of the elevator car, recorded every second for 4 seconds.

Detective 1 · Data Detective

Your Job: Call Out the Clues

Read this table out loud to your partner, one row at a time, slowly enough for her to plot each point.

Time (s)01234
Position (m)036912

Then figure out: how many meters does the elevator move during EACH one-second interval? Is that amount the same every time, or does it change?

Detective 2 · Pattern Plotter

Your Job: Plot & Describe

As your partner calls out each point, mark it on this grid (on paper, a whiteboard, or by describing dot positions out loud). Connect the dots in order.

Then describe: what shape did your line make? Straight? Curved? Did it speed up, or stay steady?

Case Verdict — Answer Together
  1. Did Mover A speed up, slow down, stay steady, or return to where it started?
  2. Invent a nickname for this movement pattern (don't use real science words — make one up!).
  3. Bonus: name one real-life "mover" that behaves exactly like this.
