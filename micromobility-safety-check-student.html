The Micromobility Safety Check

Cities across the U.S. are rewriting the rules for e-scooters and e-bikes this year — and your group is about to investigate exactly the kind of question city safety boards are asking right now.

10 minwith your group
2scenarios, one per group
No materialsneeded

The big picture

A real safety debate, happening in cities right now

In 2026, cities from Kansas City to New York to Los Angeles have all passed new speed limits, age rules, and safety requirements for e-scooters and e-bikes. One reason: emergency room visits for e-scooter and e-bike injuries among riders under 18 have risen sharply in the past few years. City officials, safety boards, and manufacturers are all trying to figure out what actually makes these rides more dangerous — and what would make them safer.

Your class is splitting into two investigation teams. Each team gets a different real safety question that a city might actually be asking. You won't need any special vocabulary today — just sharp eyes and good teamwork.

Group A

Does wheel size change top speed?

Residents have been complaining that some e-scooters seem to zip down sidewalks much faster than others. A city safety board wants to know if wheel size is the reason.

The safety board tests two scooter models on the same stretch of sidewalk: one with small 8-inch wheels, and one with larger 10-inch wheels. The same rider rides each scooter the same distance, and the board times how long each trip takes.

Role 1

The Change Spotter

Find the ONE thing the safety board is deliberately testing between the two scooters.

Report outWhat's the one thing being compared here?

Role 2

The Data Detective

Figure out exactly what the board needs to record to answer their question.

Report outWhat has to be measured on each ride? What would you need to calculate to actually compare the two scooters fairly?

Role 3

The Fairness Checker

Think about everything else about this test that needs to be identical for it to be a fair comparison.

Report outName at least two things that must stay exactly the same between the two scooter tests. What could go wrong if they didn't?

Role 4

The Field Reporter

Pull your team's three answers together into one short summary.

Report outIn one or two sentences, explain to the rest of the class what your team is testing and how.

Group B

Does a wet road change stopping distance?

City officials are worried about scooter riders crashing at crosswalks — especially in bad weather. A safety board wants to know how much wet pavement actually affects braking.

The safety board tests the same scooter, ridden by the same rider at the same starting speed, braking hard on two different surfaces: dry pavement and wet pavement. They measure how far the scooter slides before coming to a complete stop on each surface.

Role 1

The Change Spotter

Find the ONE thing the safety board is deliberately testing between the two braking tests.

Report outWhat's the one thing being compared here?

Role 2

The Data Detective

Figure out exactly what the board needs to record to answer their question.

Report outWhat has to be measured on each braking test? How would you make sure that measurement is accurate?

Role 3

The Fairness Checker

Report outName at least two things that must stay exactly the same between the two braking tests. What could go wrong if they didn't?

If your group only has 3 people

Combine the Data Detective and Field Reporter jobs — one person can handle both.

When both groups are back

Compare notes

Group A tested speed. Group B tested braking. Different questions, same city safety board — and as you'll see when both Field Reporters share out, both teams just did the exact same kind of scientific thinking to get there.
