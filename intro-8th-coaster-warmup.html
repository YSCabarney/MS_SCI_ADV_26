Coaster Warm-Up

You have been learning about distance, displacement, speed, and velocity. Now you will use those ideas to plan a roller coaster experiment. This quick activity gets your brain ready for the lab prep.

Time: 10–15 minHow: whole class on video, answers in chat
STEP 1

Spot the variables

≈ 3 min

In the lab you will build a foam-tube roller coaster and ask: How does the height a ball is released from affect its speed at the bottom of the drop, and how fast does it need to go to make a loop?

Click each item to sort it: one click = change on purpose (independent variable), two = measure (dependent variable), three = keep the same (controlled variable).

Talk about it

Why must we change only one thing at a time? What would happen to our results if we changed both the release height and the ball?

STEP 3

Predict the graph

≈ 3 min

In the lab you will release the ball from 20, 30, 40, and 50 cm and measure its speed at the bottom. Which graph do you predict? Click one, then click Reveal.

What usually happens

Speed goes up as the height goes up, but the graph curves and flattens (graph B). A taller drop gives gravity more time to speed the ball up, but friction and rolling take a bigger share as the ball goes faster. Your lab data may look a little different, and that is fine: real data are messy!

Practice numbers only (not real data): 20 cm → 1.3 m/s · 30 cm → 1.6 m/s · 40 cm → 1.9 m/s · 50 cm → 2.1 m/s.
