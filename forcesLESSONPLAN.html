Teacher Plan · Unit: Motion & Forces · Case 07

Forces Cause Motion

The next step after the Coaster Lab. Students learn why objects accelerate (net force, balanced vs. unbalanced, friction, inertia), and every new idea is tied back to the speed, velocity and acceleration skills they struggled with.

Grade 8 Physical ScienceTime 2 × 40 minClass 6 students, virtual, 3 breakout pairsNGSS MS-PS2-2
Where we are

How this builds on previous lessons

  • Motion Files, Case 04: students can calculate acceleration. Now they learn what causes it: an unbalanced force.
  • Coaster Lab Background Research: they already wrote about gravity and friction informally. This lesson makes those ideas official and measurable.
  • Coaster Lab skills: the new lab reuses the same kit, speed trap, 3 trials, AVERAGE formulas, and Google Sheet layout. That way the only new thing is the science.
  • Return Trip / Motion Detectives: same breakout-room pairs with defined roles, and the same "Motion Investigation Bureau" story (this is Case 07).
Goals

Learning objectives

By the end of Session 2, each student can:

  1. Calculate a net force and decide whether forces are balanced or unbalanced.
  2. Explain that balanced forces mean no change in velocity (even while moving), and unbalanced forces cause acceleration in the direction of the net force.
  3. Explain inertia and friction using an everyday or sports example.
  4. Use lab data to calculate speed (d ÷ t) and acceleration ((vf − vi) ÷ t), and explain what a negative acceleration means. This one is review, practiced again until it's solid.
Review plan

Built-in repetition

Since students struggled with the last section, the old skills aren't reviewed once and dropped. They come back in every part of the lesson, each time attached to the new idea. Students also hear the same 3-step recipe every time: ① formula → ② plug in → ③ answer with units.

Cheat sheet

Struggle spots to watch for

Carry-over mistakes from motion

  • Flipping the subtraction: (vi − vf) instead of (vf − vi). Have them say "final minus starting" out loud every time.
  • Mixing units: writing m/s for acceleration. Ask: "Is it a speed, or a change in speed per second?"
  • Speed vs. velocity: forgetting direction. Keep asking: "Which way?"

New force misconceptions

  • "A moving thing must have a force pushing it." No: Mission D on ice and Game Film Play 4 (the bus).
  • "Balanced means not moving." Balanced means velocity not changing, so the object could be at rest or cruising.
  • "At the top of a set, there's no force." Gravity never turns off (Game Film Play 6).

What results to expect

Starting speeds from 20 cm are usually about 1.0–1.5 m/s. A towel or thick rug stops the ball fastest: short distance, larger negative acceleration (often −1 to −3 m/s²). A hard floor gives the longest roll. If a student's acceleration comes out positive, she subtracted backwards. The "Check yourself" box on the lab page catches this.

Checklist

Materials (at home)

Session 1

  • Nothing hands-on: Session 1 is all on screen
  • Teacher: share your screen for Predict It!. Students: a device that can share its screen (the Driver in each pair, for the simulator)

Session 2

  • Coaster Lab kit: 2 half-pipes, the same ball, painter's tape, tape measure
  • Books for a 20 cm ramp
  • 3 surfaces (towel, rug, yoga mat, blanket, hard floor)
  • A phone stopwatch with a Lap button
Assessment

How you'll know they got it

  • Formative: Rewind chat answers (check who's still flipping the subtraction), mission report-outs, Game Film B/U calls, and the exit ticket.
  • Homework: 10 new cards with "Connects to" lines, 4 upgraded old cards, and the student's ★ list of cards she missed.
  • Lab: the Sheet (formulas correct, every acceleration negative), 8 analysis questions, and the CER conclusion.

Adjusting the difficulty

  • Extra support: pair a struggling student with a confident partner as Recorder in Session 1, so she sees each step. The sentence starters are already built into the lab questions.
  • Extra challenge: in the sim, predict the acceleration with a = net force ÷ mass before running it, then check against the "velocity every second" row. In the lab, use v2 = 2ad to see whether the stopping distance matches the calculated acceleration.
