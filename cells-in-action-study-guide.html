Part 1 — Organelle Review

You learned these — now let's connect them to what cells actually DO.

Remember: Every organelle has a specific job. The shape of an organelle is related to its function — this is called structure-function relationship.

As you read through these, ask yourself: "How does this organelle's job connect to making energy or building something?"

🧬 Nucleus
Holds your DNA — the instruction manual for everything the cell does. Controls when to make proteins and when to divide.
Control center
⚡ Mitochondria
Makes ATP — the energy currency of the cell. Folded inner membranes give more surface area for energy production. You'll see this in cellular respiration!
⭐ Key for today
🔧 Ribosome
Tiny factories that build proteins using instructions from DNA. There are thousands in each cell. Found on rough ER or floating free.
Protein maker
🚪 Cell Membrane
The gatekeeper. Selectively permeable — it chooses what can pass in or out. Made of a double layer of lipids with proteins.
Transport
📦 Golgi Apparatus
The cell's post office. Receives proteins from the ER, packages them, labels them, and ships them to where they're needed.
Shipping dept.
🌀 Endoplasmic Reticulum
Rough ER has ribosomes on it (builds proteins). Smooth ER makes lipids and detoxifies the cell. A maze of connected channels.
Transport highway
🫙 Vacuole
A storage sac. In plant cells, the central vacuole is huge and filled with water — it keeps the plant stiff and upright. Animal cells have smaller vacuoles.
Storage
🗑️ Lysosome
The cell's cleanup crew. Contains enzymes that break down old, worn-out organelles and waste products. Only in animal cells.
Animal only
🌿 Chloroplast
Plant cells only! Captures sunlight and uses it to make sugar (glucose) through photosynthesis. Contains the green pigment chlorophyll.
⭐ Key for today
🧱 Cell Wall
Plant cells only! A rigid outer layer outside the cell membrane. Made of cellulose. Provides extra support and protection.
Plant only

Part 2 — Photosynthesis

How plants capture sunlight and make food.

🌿 What Is Photosynthesis?

Photosynthesis is how plant cells (and some algae) make their own food using sunlight. The chloroplast captures light energy and uses it to convert two simple ingredients into sugar.

The word equation:

☀️ Sunlight + 💧 Water + 🌬️ Carbon Dioxide → 🍬 Glucose + 💨 Oxygen

The chemical equation:

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

🔄 Step-by-Step: How It Happens

☀️ Sunlight hits leaf Energy captured by chlorophyll
→
🌿 Chloroplast activates Inside the plant cell
→
💧+🌬️ Water + CO₂ used Raw materials go in
→
🍬+💨 Glucose + O₂ made Products come out

💡 The oxygen that plants release is what we breathe. The glucose is stored as food energy — for the plant, and for animals that eat the plant.

🔬 Inside the Chloroplast

  • The chloroplast has a double membrane — like a bag inside a bag
  • Thylakoids — flat, disc-like structures stacked in columns called grana. This is where light energy is captured.
  • Stroma — the fluid inside the chloroplast where the sugar-making reactions happen
  • Chlorophyll — the green pigment inside thylakoids that absorbs red and blue light (and reflects green — that's why leaves look green!)

Part 3 — Cellular Respiration

How cells break down sugar to release usable energy (ATP).

⚡ What Is Cellular Respiration?

Cellular respiration is how all living cells (plants AND animals) release energy from glucose. It happens in the mitochondria and produces ATP — the molecule that powers everything you do.

The word equation:

🍬 Glucose + 💨 Oxygen → ⚡ ATP Energy + 💧 Water + 🌬️ Carbon Dioxide

The chemical equation:

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → ATP + 6H₂O + 6CO₂

💰 What Is ATP?

Think of ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate) like a cell's currency. Energy doesn't go directly from glucose to a muscle — it gets converted into ATP first, and then ATP powers the work.

  • One glucose molecule can produce about 36-38 ATP molecules
  • ATP is used for everything: moving muscles, building proteins, sending signals
  • When ATP releases energy, it becomes ADP — and the cell recharges it back to ATP again

🔄 Step-by-Step: Cellular Respiration

🍬 Glucose arrives From food or photosynthesis
→
💨 Oxygen comes in You breathe it in
→
⚡ Mitochondria react Break glucose down
→
💪 ATP released Powers cell work!

🔬 Why Does the Mitochondria Have Folds?

The inner membrane of the mitochondria is highly folded — these folds are called cristae. More folds = more surface area = more space for chemical reactions = MORE ATP produced!

This is a perfect example of how structure supports function — the shape of the mitochondria is directly related to its job of making energy.

Part 4 — The Big Connection

How photosynthesis and cellular respiration are opposites — and partners.

🔁 They're Basically Opposite Reactions!

🌱 Photosynthesis ⚡ Cellular Respiration
Where?ChloroplastMitochondria
Who does it?Plants (+ algae)ALL living cells
Uses upCO₂ + H₂O + sunlightGlucose + O₂
ProducesGlucose + O₂ATP + CO₂ + H₂O
Energy directionStores energyReleases energy
🔁

The cycle: Plants make glucose (photosynthesis) → Animals eat plants → Animals use glucose for energy (cellular respiration) → CO₂ and H₂O are released → Plants use CO₂ again for photosynthesis. It's a perfect loop!

Part 5 — Volleyball + Your Cells

Why your body works the way it does during a game.

🏐 What's Happening in Your Body Right Now?

When you spike the ball: Your arm muscle cells need a huge burst of ATP. Mitochondria in those cells ramp up cellular respiration at high speed.
Why you breathe harder: More cellular respiration = more oxygen needed + more CO₂ produced. Your breathing speeds up to supply O₂ and remove CO₂.
Why muscles burn: When your muscles work faster than oxygen can arrive, they switch to a backup system (anaerobic respiration) — lactic acid builds up and causes that burning feeling.
Why you eat before a game: Food → digested into glucose → glucose stored in muscles → cellular respiration converts it to ATP → you can jump, run, spike!
Why plant foods are great fuel: Plants are glucose-making machines (photosynthesis). When you eat vegetables, fruits, or grains, you're using the energy that the sun put into the plant — now powering your jump serve.

Part 6 — Big Ideas to Remember

✅ Key Takeaways from This Unit

✓

Every organelle has a specific structure that matches its function — shape = job.

✓

Photosynthesis happens in chloroplasts — plants capture sunlight to make glucose and release oxygen.

✓

Cellular respiration happens in mitochondria — cells break down glucose into ATP energy, releasing CO₂ and water.

✓

These two processes are nearly opposite reactions — together they cycle matter and energy through all living things.

✓

ATP is the energy currency of every cell — it powers muscle contractions, protein building, and more.

✓

When you eat, breathe, move, or even just exist — cellular respiration is happening in every one of your trillions of cells.
