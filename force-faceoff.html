Part 1

Rewind: Motion Review

6 min · type answers in the chat

Quick reminder: 4 ideas you already know

Distance vs. displacementDistance = the whole path you traveled. Displacement = a straight line from start to finish.
Speed vs. velocitySpeed = how fast. Velocity = how fast and which way.speed = distance ÷ time
AccelerationHow much the velocity changes each second.a = (final velocity − starting velocity) ÷ time
+ or − accelerationPositive (+) = speeding up. Negative (−) = slowing down.

Use the 3-step recipe on every math problem

① Write the formula  →  ② Put in the numbers  →  ③ Write the answer with units

Rewind 1 · Distance vs. displacementA volleyball player runs from the end line to the net (9 m). Then she jogs back to the end line. What is her distance? What is her displacement?
Need a hint?

Distance counts every step. Displacement only asks: how far is she from where she started?

Show answer

Distance = 9 m + 9 m = 18 m. Displacement = 0 m, because she ended right where she started.

Rewind 2 · SpeedA ball rolls 0.80 m in 0.40 seconds. What is its speed?
Show the 3 steps
① Formulaspeed = distance ÷ time② Numbersspeed = 0.80 m ÷ 0.40 s③ Answer2.0 m/s
Rewind 3 · Speed vs. velocityA runner sprints one full lap around a track and ends where she started. Her speed was 5 m/s. What was her velocity?
Need a hint?

Velocity uses displacement. What is her displacement if she ended where she started?

Show answer

0 m/s. Her displacement was 0 m, so her velocity was 0 m/s, even though she ran fast the whole time.

Rewind 4 · Speeding upA soccer player goes from 2 m/s to 6 m/s in 2 seconds. What is her acceleration?
Show the 3 steps
① Formulaa = (final − starting) ÷ time② Numbersa = (6 − 2) ÷ 2 = 4 ÷ 2③ Answer2 m/s² → positive, so she's speeding up
Rewind 5 · Slowing downA ball is rolling at 3 m/s. It stops in 1.5 seconds. What is its acceleration?
Need a hint?

"Stops" means the final velocity is 0. Always do final minus starting: (0 − 3).

Show the 3 steps
① Formulaa = (final − starting) ÷ time② Numbersa = (0 − 3) ÷ 1.5 = −3 ÷ 1.5③ Answer−2 m/s² → negative, so it's slowing down
Rewind 6 · Think about itA ball goes around a loop at the same speed the whole time. Is it accelerating?
Need a hint?

Acceleration means the velocity changes. Velocity = speed and direction. Does the direction change in a loop?

Show answer

Yes. Its direction keeps changing, so its velocity keeps changing. That means it's accelerating.

Round 2

Tug-of-War

7 min · breakout rooms

First, learn 4 new words

ForceA push or a pull.
Newton (N)The unit we use to measure force. "50 N" means a pull of 50 newtons.
Net forceWhat's left over after the forces work against each other. It's the force that "wins."
Balanced / UnbalancedBalanced = net force is 0 (a tie). Unbalanced = net force is not 0 (someone wins).

How to find the net force

When two teams pull in opposite directions, subtract. Big number minus small number. The bigger team wins.

Left pulls 30 NRight pulls 50 N→50 − 30 = 20 N to the right

If both teams pull the same, the answer is 0 N. That's a tie, so the forces are balanced.

Every pair · do this first

Mission A · Make a Tie

Your goal: both teams pull, but the cart does not move.

Steps: use the − / + buttons to change the pulls. Neither team can be 0. Find 2 different ways to do it.

Left ___ N and Right ___ N made a tie. The net force was ___ N.
Check your answer

Any two equal pulls work, like 30 N and 30 N. The net force is 0 N, so the forces are balanced, and the cart does not move.

Pair 1

Mission B · Speed Up by 1

Your goal: make the velocity go up by 1 m/s every second: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.

Steps: change the pulls and press Run. Look at the "Velocity every second" row. Keep trying until it counts up by 1.

Then do the math: a = (final velocity − starting velocity) ÷ time = (5 − 0) ÷ 5 = ___

We used Left ___ N and Right ___ N. The net force was ___ N. The acceleration was ___ m/s².
Check your answer

A net force of 20 N works, like Left 20 and Right 40. The velocity goes 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Acceleration = (5 − 0) ÷ 5 = 1 m/s².

Pair 2

Mission C · Heavy Cart

Your goal: find out what happens when the cart is heavier but the pull stays the same.

Steps: (1) Press Run with the 20 kg cart. Write down the acceleration. (2) Click 40 kg. Press Run again. Write down the acceleration.

20 kg: ___ m/s². 40 kg: ___ m/s². The heavier cart sped up (more / less).
Check your answer

The net force is 40 N both times. The 20 kg cart: 2 m/s². The 40 kg cart: 1 m/s². A heavier object speeds up less with the same force.

Pair 3

Mission D · Let Go!

Your goal: find out what the cart does when nobody is pulling anymore.

Steps: (1) On Ice, press Run. When the clock shows about 2 seconds, press ✋ Drop the ropes. Watch the cart. (2) Click Grass and do it again.

On ice, after the drop, the cart ___. On grass, after the drop, the cart ___.
Check your answer

On ice: the cart keeps rolling at the same speed. There's no friction, so nothing slows it down. The dots stay evenly spaced.

On grass: the cart slows down and stops. Friction pulls backward, so the dots bunch together.

The Big Idea

What We Learned

1 min
Rule 1If the net force is 0 (balanced), the motion does not change. A still object stays still. A moving object keeps moving at the same speed.
Rule 2If the net force is not 0 (unbalanced), the object accelerates: it speeds up, slows down, or turns. A heavier object accelerates less.
InertiaThings keep doing what they're already doing until a force changes it. That's why the volleyball kept rolling when the van braked, why the dishes stayed on the table, and why the cart kept rolling on the ice.
Part 3

Game Film: Balanced or Unbalanced?

9 min · whole class

How to play: read each play and ask, "Is the motion changing?" No → type B (balanced). Yes → type U (unbalanced). Then click to check.

Play 1A volleyball sits on the gym floor during a timeout.
Check

B: balanced. The ball is not moving and stays not moving. Gravity pulls it down, and the floor pushes it up just as hard. It's a tie.

Play 2A soccer ball rolls across the grass and slowly stops.
Check

U: unbalanced. The ball is slowing down. Friction from the grass pulls backward, and nothing pushes it forward.

Play 3A hitter's hand smacks the ball during a spike.
Check

U: unbalanced. The ball speeds up a lot, very fast. The hand's push is much bigger than anything else.

Play 4 · Careful!The team bus drives down a straight highway at a steady 55 mph.
Check

B: balanced! The bus is moving, but its motion is not changing: same speed, same direction. The engine's push is a tie with the forces pushing back. Moving does not always mean unbalanced.

Play 5You and a teammate push on opposite sides of a door, exactly as hard as each other.
Check

B: balanced. Equal pushes in opposite directions make a tie, so the door doesn't move. It's just like Mission A.

Play 6 · ChallengeA volleyball at the very top of a high set, for the instant it isn't going up or down.
Check

U: unbalanced. Gravity is still pulling down, and nothing is holding the ball up. That's why it starts to fall right away.
