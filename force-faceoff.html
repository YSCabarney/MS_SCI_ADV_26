Meet Newton

About 350 years ago, a scientist named Isaac Newton figured out the rules for how forces change motion. Today you'll meet his ideas, then see each one in action.

The newton (N): how we measure force

A force is a push or a pull. We measure forces in newtons, written N, named after Isaac Newton. Here's about how hard Earth pulls down on some everyday things:

Newton's 1st Law

Things keep doing what they're doing

A still object stays still, and a moving object keeps moving the same way, unless a force changes it. This is called inertia.

A soccer ball sits still until you kick it.

See it in Examples 1, 3, 5

Newton's 2nd Law

Force makes things speed up

A bigger force makes an object speed up more. A bigger mass makes it speed up less.

A hard spike sends the ball flying faster than a soft tip.

See it in Examples 2, 4

Newton's 3rd Law

Every push gets pushed back

When you push on something, it pushes back on you just as hard, in the opposite direction.

When you jump, you push down on the floor, and the floor pushes you up.

See it in Example 4

Example 2 · Net force

Tug-of-War Showdown

Team Left pulls the cart with 30 N. Team Right pulls with 50 N. The cart is on ice. What happens?

ANothing. Both teams are pulling, so it's a tie.
BIt moves left.
CIt speeds up to the right.

① Type A, B, or C in the chat. ② Watch what happens.

It speeds up to the right. The forces work against each other: 50 N − 30 N = 20 N to the right. That leftover force is the net force. When the net force isn't zero, the forces are unbalanced, and the cart speeds up. Look at the dots spreading apart.

Newton's 2nd Law The net force makes the cart speed up: 20 N on a 20 kg cart gives 20 ÷ 20 = 1 m/s every second. Check the "Velocity every second" row: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5!

Your turn: use the − / + buttons to make it a tie, with both teams still pulling. What's the net force now? (Answer: 0 N. Equal pulls make the forces balanced, so the cart doesn't move.)

The Team Bus

On the way to a tournament, the team bus cruises down a straight highway at a steady 55 mph. Its engine is pushing it forward. Are the forces on the bus balanced or unbalanced?

ABalanced
BUnbalanced. It's moving, so the forward force must be winning.

① Type A or B in the chat. ② Be ready to explain why.

Balanced! The bus is moving, but its motion isn't changing: same speed, same direction. The engine's forward push exactly matches friction and air pushing back, so the net force is 0 N.

Newton's 1st Law Just like the volleyball in Example 1, a moving object keeps moving with no net force. That's inertia. Moving doesn't automatically mean unbalanced.

Wrap-up

The Big Idea

Rule 1 · Balanced (net force = 0) · Newton's 1st LawThe motion does not change. A still object stays still, and a moving object keeps the same speed and direction.
Rule 2 · Unbalanced (net force ≠ 0) · Newton's 2nd LawThe object accelerates: it speeds up, slows down, or turns. Friction slows things down, and more mass means less acceleration.
InertiaThings keep doing what they're doing until a force changes it. (Example 1)
Net forceThe force left over after the forces work against each other. (Example 2)
FrictionA force that rubs against motion and slows it down. (Example 3)
MassHow much "stuff" an object has. More mass, less acceleration. (Example 4)
Newton (N)The unit for force. A small apple weighs about 1 N.
3rd LawEvery push gets pushed back just as hard. (Example 4)
