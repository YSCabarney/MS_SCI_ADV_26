Option A · Inclined plane

The Parade Float Ramp

Your town’s Thanksgiving parade float has to get pushed up a ramp onto a trailer. You’re the engineer. Design the float, then decide: short steep ramp, or long gradual one?

Given data

QuantityValue
Float weight (the load)24,000 N
Trailer height1.5 m
Ramp option 1 (gradual)6.0 m long
Ramp option 2 (steep)3.0 m long
One volunteer can push500 N

Design rules

  • Your float must be your own original design, not a character from a movie, show, or game.
  • Draw the float on the ramp, with the ramp’s length and height labeled.
  • Draw an effort force arrow showing which way the volunteers push.
  • Show both ramp options, side by side or one above the other.

See it in action

Here’s my float. Switch ramps and watch two things: how big the push arrow is, and how far the float has to travel.

Effort (the push) Distance pushed

Gradual ramp: a smaller push, but a longer trip.

Part 1: Compare the ramps

Like Pair A, tasks 1–2
  1. Calculate the mechanical advantage of each ramp.
  2. Calculate the effort force needed to push the float up each ramp. Assume no friction.
  3. Each volunteer can push 500 N. How many volunteers does each ramp need?
MA = ramp length ÷ height. Then Effort = Load ÷ MA. For volunteers, divide the effort force by 500 N. A bigger MA should give you a smaller effort.

Part 2: The work question

Like Pair A, task 4
  1. Use W = F × d to find the work done pushing the float up each ramp. Use the effort force for F and the ramp length for d.
  2. Now find the work to lift the float straight up 1.5 m with no ramp at all (F = 24,000 N).
  3. Compare all three answers. What do you notice? What does this tell you about what a ramp really does?
Work comes out in newton-meters (N·m), also called joules (J). When the force goes down but the distance goes up, what happens to force × distance?

Engineer’s decision

Like Pair A, task 3

Which ramp would you build, and why? Write at least two reasons on your poster.

Optional bonus, just for bragging rights: only 10 volunteers show up. How long would the ramp need to be so 10 people can push the float up? Add it to your poster.

The menu: pick one dish

Ramp · like Pair A

Pumpkin Wagon Pie

A farmer pushes a wagon full of pumpkins weighing 1,600 N up a 3.2 m ramp into a truck bed that’s 0.8 m high.

Find the MA and the effort force. Think about it: what happens to the effort if the farmer uses a 1.6 m ramp instead?

Lever · like Pair B

Stuck-Table Stuffing

Grandma’s dining table extension is jammed shut. It takes 1,200 N to pry it open, but you can only push with 200 N. You grab a Class 1 lever.

What MA do you need? Think about it: if the load side of your lever is 0.1 m long, how long does the effort side need to be?

Pulley · like Pair C

Turkey Fryer Fricassee

A turkey in its fryer basket weighs 240 N. A pulley lifts it 1.5 m out of the fryer. You can safely pull with 60 N. (Adults only. Hot oil is no joke.)

What MA do you need? How much rope do you pull? Think about it: with an MA of only 3, could you still lift it?

Pumpkin wagon: MA = ramp length ÷ height, then Effort = Load ÷ MA. Do it again with the shorter ramp and compare.

Stuck table: MA = Load ÷ Effort. An MA of 6 means the effort side has to be 6 times longer than the load side.

Turkey fryer: MA = Load ÷ Effort. Rope pulled = lift height × MA. For MA = 3, find Effort = Load ÷ 3 and compare it to 60 N.
