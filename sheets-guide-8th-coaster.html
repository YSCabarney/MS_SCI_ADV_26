Step 3

Write the Average Time Formula

  1. Click cell E2.
  2. Type =AVERAGE(B2:D2) and press Enter. This averages the three raw times in that row.
  3. Click E2 again, grab the tiny blue square in its bottom-right corner (the fill handle), and drag it down through E5 — this copies the formula to every row automatically, adjusting the row numbers for you.

Step 4

Write the Speed Formula

  1. Click cell G2.
  2. Type =F2/E2 and press Enter — trap length divided by average time gives you speed.
  3. Drag the fill handle from G2 down through G5, same as before.

Step 5

Select Your Data and Insert a Chart

  1. Click cell A1, then hold Ctrl (Windows) or Cmd (Mac) and click-drag to also select column G — this lets you grab two columns that aren't next to each other.
  2. Go to Insert → Chart in the menu bar.
  3. A default chart appears, and the Chart editor panel opens on the right.

Configure the Chart

  1. On the Setup tab: set Chart type to Scatter chart. Confirm the X-axis shows Release height — if it's backwards, use "Switch rows/columns."
  2. Switch to the Customize tab. Under Chart & axis titles, add a chart title, an X-axis title (Release height (cm)), and a Y-axis title (Average speed (m/s)).
  3. Still on Customize, open Series and turn on Trendline.
💡

If your chart looks like a jagged connect-the-dots line instead of separate points, you picked Line chart instead of Scatter — go back to Setup and change the Chart type.

Result

What You Should End Up With

Four separate dots, rising left to right in a steady, nearly straight pattern, with a dashed trendline sloping upward through them — no zig-zag lines connecting point to point.

Why It's Set Up This Way

QuestionAnswer
Why is release height on the x-axis?It's the independent variable — the thing you changed on purpose.
Why is average speed on the y-axis?It's the dependent variable — the thing you measured, which responds to the change.
What does the pattern mean?As release height increases, average speed increases in a steady, nearly straight pattern.
