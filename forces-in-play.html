Quick reference — new words, plain definitions

Independent variable
The one thing you deliberately change on purpose. In your lab: ramp height or angle.
Dependent variable
The thing you measure because it responds to that change. In your lab: speed.
Gravity
The constant downward pull on every object — it's what gives a ramp (or a ski jump) something to speed up into.
Friction
A force between two surfaces in contact that resists motion and slows things down.

Listen with headphones if you can. Every time you hear friction, gravity, energy, or a scientist describing a test, pause and jot one word about it — you'll want that note for Step 02.

02

Think & answerplan your answers before you record

Question 1 · Variables & testing

In your ramp lab, you changed the height or angle on purpose and measured how it changed speed. In the podcast, scientists study athletes the same way. Pick one example from the episode and explain: what was being changed, what was being measured, and how is that the same kind of test you ran with your ramp?

Try to include: the words "independent variable" and "dependent variable," one specific example from the podcast (a sport, athlete, or piece of gear), and one specific number or observation from your own lab data.
Question 2 · Gravity & friction

Gravity pulls everything down; friction pushes back against motion. Using your own lab data as evidence, explain how a steeper angle or greater height changed your object's speed. Then connect it to gravity or friction at work in the podcast — and to something you notice about speed, jumping, or striking a ball in your own sport.

Try to include: at least one real number from your data table, the words "gravity" and "friction" used correctly, and one connection to volleyball or soccer.
Question 3 · Why testing matters

Scientists — and athletes — don't just guess and stop. They test, collect data, then test again to get better. Why did it matter that your lab tested more than one height or angle instead of just one? Use a specific idea from the podcast to explain why testing and analyzing data matters for athletes, not just scientists — and how you could use that same thinking to improve in your own sport.

Try to include: a reason more than one trial makes data more trustworthy, one idea from the podcast, and one concrete way you could "test and adjust" in practice or training.
