Part 1

Make 10 New Cards

You need: 20 index cards (2 for each word), a glue stick or tape, and markers.

  1. Card 1 (front): write the word. Make it big and bold.
  2. Card 2 (back): write what the word means, in your own words. Don't copy it exactly. Change it so it makes sense to you.
  3. Also on Card 2: at the bottom, write "Connects to:" and the old motion word it goes with. Copy it from the teal box on each card below.
  4. Glue Card 1 and Card 2 back-to-back.
  5. Post it on your Word Wall, right next to the old card it connects to.

Your 10 words

The Words

01

Force

A push or a pull.

ExampleYour hand hitting the ball when you serve.

Connects to: VectorA force has a size and a direction, just like velocity.

02

Newton (N)

The unit we use to measure force. 1 newton is about how hard a small apple presses down on your hand.

Example"The team pulled with 50 N."

Connects to: m/sSpeed is measured in m/s. Force is measured in N.

03

Net Force

The force that's left over after all the forces work against each other. It's the force that "wins."

ExampleRight pulls 50 N, left pulls 30 N. Net force = 50 − 30 = 20 N to the right.

Connects to: DisplacementBoth care about direction: which way?

04

Balanced Forces

Forces that make a tie, so the net force is 0. The motion does not change: a still object stays still, and a moving object keeps the same speed and direction.

ExampleA tug-of-war where nobody moves.

Connects to: Constant VelocityBalanced forces mean the velocity stays the same.

05

Unbalanced Forces

Forces that don't make a tie, so the net force is not 0. The object speeds up, slows down, or turns.

ExampleA soccer ball slowing down on the grass.

Connects to: AccelerationUnbalanced forces are what cause acceleration.

06

Gravity

The force that pulls things down toward the Earth.

ExampleA set always comes back down to the hitter.

Connects to: The Coaster LabGravity made your ball speed up going down the drop.

07

Friction

A force that rubs against something moving and slows it down. It always pushes the opposite way from the motion.

ExampleYour court shoes grip the floor so you can stop fast.

Connects to: DecelerationFriction is what makes things slow down.

08

Mass

How much "stuff" is in an object. We measure it in kilograms (kg). More mass means the object is harder to speed up or slow down.

ExampleA medicine ball is harder to throw than a volleyball.

Connects to: AccelerationSame push + more mass = less acceleration.

09

Inertia

Things keep doing what they're already doing. A still object stays still, and a moving object keeps moving, until a force changes it.

ExampleYou lurch forward when the bus stops suddenly. Your body wants to keep moving.

Connects to: VelocityWithout an unbalanced force, velocity stays the same.

10

Newton's Second Law

A bigger force makes more acceleration. More mass makes less acceleration. As a formula: acceleration = net force ÷ mass.

ExampleA 40 N net force on a 20 kg cart: 40 ÷ 20 = 2 m/s².

Connects to: AccelerationA second way to find acceleration.

Part 3

Self-Check

5 min

Try each question before you click. Use the hint if you get stuck.

Check 1 · Net forceTeam Left pulls 40 N. Team Right pulls 70 N. What is the net force? Is it balanced or unbalanced?
Hint

Opposite directions, so subtract: big number minus small number.

Answer

70 − 40 = 30 N to the right. It's not 0, so it's unbalanced.

Check 2 · Balanced or not?A hockey puck slides across ice at a steady 3 m/s. Nothing slows it down. Are the forces balanced?
Hint

Is the puck speeding up, slowing down, or turning?

Answer

Balanced. Its motion isn't changing, so the net force is 0. It keeps sliding because of inertia.

Check 3 · Acceleration mathA ball slows from 4 m/s to 0 m/s in 2 seconds. What is its acceleration?
Hint

a = (final − starting) ÷ time. Final = 0, starting = 4.

Answer

a = (0 − 4) ÷ 2 = −2 m/s². It's negative because the ball is slowing down. Friction caused it.

Check 4 · Newton's Second LawThe same 60 N net force pushes a 20 kg cart and a 30 kg cart. Which one speeds up more?
Hint

acceleration = net force ÷ mass. Do it for both carts.

Answer

20 kg: 60 ÷ 20 = 3 m/s². 30 kg: 60 ÷ 30 = 2 m/s². The lighter cart speeds up more.

Check 5 · Distance and displacementA ball rolls 1.2 m in a straight line and stops. What is its distance? What is its displacement?
Hint

Did the ball ever turn around?

Answer

Both are 1.2 m. In a straight line with no turning back, distance and displacement are the same.
