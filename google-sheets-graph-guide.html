Graphing in Google Sheets

Step-by-step help for making a table, doing math with formulas, and turning your data into a graph. Both the 7th grade Scale Lab and the 8th grade Roller Coaster Lab use these steps.

Time: 15–20 min to learn You need: a Chromebook and your school Google account
A · Set up B · Formulas C · Make a chart D · Fix up your chart E · Put it in your report F · Checklist
Which chart do I make?

7th grade (Scale Lab): a column chart that compares things in categories (like salt, hair, and paper towel). Use the Example 1 data.

8th grade (Roller Coaster Lab): a scatter chart with a trendline that shows how one number changes when another number changes (release height vs. speed). Use the Example 2 data.

The steps are almost the same. Only the chart type changes.

The one graphing rule to remember

The independent variable (the thing you change on purpose) goes on the x-axis (across the bottom).
The dependent variable (the thing you measure) goes on the y-axis (up the side).

PART A

Set up your spreadsheet

≈ 3 min

  1. Open Google Drive. Click + New, then Google Sheets, then Blank spreadsheet. (You can also go to sheets.google.com and click the big +.)

  2. Click the words Untitled spreadsheet at the top left and give it a name, like Scale Lab Data – Team Name. Press Enter.

  3. Type your column headings in Row 1. Put one variable in each column and include its units in parentheses.

    • Good: Release height (cm), Average speed (m/s)
    • Not good: Data, Numbers, Stuff

  4. Type your data underneath, one row per measurement. Type only the number in each data cell (no units, no words). Do not leave blank rows or columns in the middle of your data.

  5. Share it with your team. Click the green Share button (top right), type your teammates' school emails, choose Editor, and click Send.

PART B

Let the spreadsheet do the math (formulas)

≈ 4 min

A formula is a math instruction. It always starts with an equals sign (=). Instead of typing numbers, you click on the cells you want to use, so the answer updates by itself if you fix a number.

  1. Click an empty cell where you want the answer. Type =

  2. Type the math using cell names. A cell's name is its column letter and row number (the cell in column B, row 2 is B2).

    • Divide: =E2/D2
    • Multiply: =H2*1000
    • Average of several cells: =AVERAGE(G2:I2) (the colon means "from G2 to I2")
    • Percent difference between two numbers: =ABS(B2-C2)/AVERAGE(B2,C2)*100

  3. Press Enter. The answer appears.

  4. Copy the formula down the column: click the cell with your formula. A small blue square appears in its bottom-right corner. Double-click that square (or drag it down) to fill the rest of the column.

If you see an error
  • #DIV/0! means you divided by an empty cell or by zero. Fill in the number you divided by.
  • #NAME? means Sheets does not understand a word. Check for typos (AVERAGE, not "AVERGE").
  • A number that looks like text and will not add up? Make sure you did not type units (like "cm") inside the cell.
PART C

Make the chart

≈ 5 min

  1. Highlight your data. Click the top-left cell of your table (the first heading) and drag to the bottom-right cell. Include the heading row.

    • Scatter chart: highlight just the two columns you want (x-column first, y-column second).
    • Column chart: highlight the object names and the columns you want to compare.

  2. Click Insert in the menu bar, then click Chart. A chart appears and a Chart editor opens on the right side of your screen.

  3. In the Chart editor, make sure the Setup tab is selected. Click the Chart type drop-down and choose:

    • 7th grade: Column chart
    • 8th grade: Scatter chart

  4. Check the Data range box shows the cells you highlighted. Check that X-axis is your independent variable (for the 8th grade lab: release height). If the chart looks wrong, try the checkbox Use row 1 as headers, or the Switch rows / columns checkbox.

These drawings are illustrations. Your screen may look a little different because Google updates Sheets often, but the words (Insert, Chart, Setup, Customize) will be the same.

PART D

Fix up your chart so anyone can read it

≈ 5 min

  1. In the Chart editor, click the Customize tab.

  2. Click Chart & axis titles to open it. Use the drop-down under it to pick Chart title. Click the box below and type a title that says what the graph shows.

    • Good title: Release Height vs. Speed at the Bottom of the Drop
    • Not good title: Graph 1

  3. Use the same drop-down to pick Horizontal axis title and type your independent variable with units (example: Release height (cm)).

  4. Pick Vertical axis title and type your dependent variable with units (example: Average speed (m/s)).

  5. Scatter chart only — add a trendline. In Customize, click Series to open it, then check the box Trendline. Under the trendline, set Label to Use Equation and check Show R² if you want. The trendline is the "best fit" line through your dots. Do not connect the dots with a zig-zag line.

  6. Column chart only: under Legend, make sure the legend is showing so a reader can tell which color is the Low setting and which is the High setting.

  7. Drag the chart's corners to make it bigger so it is easy to read.

PART E

Put your graph in your lab report

≈ 2 min

  1. Click once on your chart. Click the three dots (⋮) that appear in its top-right corner.

  2. Choose Copy chart.

  3. Open your lab report in Google Docs. Click where the graph should go and press Ctrl + V.

  4. A box asks how to paste the chart. Choose Paste unlinked so your final graph will not change by accident.

  5. Backup plan: click the three dots (⋮), choose Download or Save image, then in Docs use Insert → Image → Upload from computer.

  6. Under your graph, type a caption: a sentence that says what the graph shows.
