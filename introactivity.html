Round 1

The Clean Play

A serve comes in. Each of you clicks your own touch, in order, and reads your job out loud to the group before the next player goes.

Touch 1 · Passer

Serve-receive

I read the serve and control it with a clean, high pass to my Setter. Nobody else can take this touch for me.
Touch 2 · Setter

The set

I take whatever the Passer gives me and turn it into a hittable ball for my Hitter. I can't set what I never receive.
Touch 3 · Hitter

The attack

I finish the play by attacking whatever the Setter puts up. I can't hit a ball I never get.
🎉 Point scored! All three jobs got done — the whole play worked.

Touch 3 · Hitter

❌ Point lost — even though the Setter and Hitter were both ready and able to do their jobs perfectly.
Say it back, all together: "The Setter and Hitter didn't fail. So why did the whole team still lose the point?" (Because every job depends on the one before it — one job failing is enough to break the whole system.)
Round 3

Zoom In

That was one rally, with 3 jobs, run by 3 players. Now guess these out loud before you reveal them.

One single tiny drop of blood — how many living, working pieces do you think are packed inside it? ≈ 250 million
Your entire body, right now — how many of those same tiny living pieces do you think you're made of? ≈ 37 trillion
New Vocabulary Cell

A cell is the smallest living piece of any living thing — and every single one has its own specific job to do, just like you on the court. You are made of trillions of them, working at the exact same time, right now.

Final Huddle

🏐

Your rally had 3 jobs. Your body has trillions — but the rule is exactly the same: each one has ONE specific job, and no other piece can do it instead.

🔗

One job failing is enough to break the whole system. Just like your Setter and Hitter couldn't save the point when the pass failed, the rest of your body depends on every single cell doing its own job correctly.
