SETUP · 2 min

What's Living Inside You

Right now, while you're reading this, there are about 38 trillion bacteria living inside your gut — your intestines. That's roughly the same number as all the human cells in your body.

Most of them are there to help you. They digest food your body can't break down on its own, make vitamins, and keep harmful bacteria from moving in. Think of them like a team — different bacteria do different jobs.

The big question: what you eat is what they eat. Feed them the right stuff and your team thrives. Feed them junk and the wrong bacteria start winning.

Your question for today: Does what you eat actually change which gut bacteria win? Each person builds a meal and watches what happens to the gut team. At the end, you compare results.
MEET THE TEAM

Five Bacteria. Two Sides.

Your gut has thousands of bacteria species, but researchers have identified a handful that consistently matter most for health. Here are the five you'll be tracking today — three on your side, two working against you.

SEM image — Lactobacillus acidophilus · CDC / Wikimedia Commons (public domain)
🛡️
Lactobacillus
The Defender

Produces lactic acid, which makes the gut too acidic for harmful bacteria to survive. This is the same bacteria found in yogurt — which is why yogurt helps your gut. Without it, harmful bacteria can move in and take over.

🌿 Thrives on: fiber, fermented foods like yogurt
🚫 Killed by: sugar, antibiotics, processed food
Bifidobacterium longum · Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA)
⚙️
Bifidobacterium
The Supplier

Breaks down fiber that your stomach can't digest on its own and turns it into vitamins B and K that your body needs. It also feeds other good bacteria, acting like a supply chain for the whole gut team.

🌿 Thrives on: fiber, fruits, beans, whole grains
🚫 Killed by: high-sugar diets, junk food
Bacteroides fragilis Gram stain · CDC / Wikimedia Commons (public domain)
🧠
Bacteroides
The Immune Trainer

Teaches your immune system the difference between safe bacteria and dangerous ones. Without it, your immune system can overreact and attack things it shouldn't — causing inflammation, fatigue, and getting sick more easily.

🌿 Thrives on: plant-based foods, vegetables
🚫 Drops with: high-fat, low-fiber diets
C. perfringens Gram stain · CDC / Wikimedia Commons (public domain)
⚠️
C. perfringens
The Threat

Normally kept at low levels by the good team. But when good bacteria are wiped out, C. perfringens explodes in population and releases toxins that cause inflammation, stomach pain, and can contribute to disease. It loves sugar and processed food.

⚡ Thrives on: sugar, processed food, red meat
✅ Kept in check by: Lactobacillus and fiber
Firmicutes member (Bacillus subtilis) · Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA)
📉
Firmicutes
The Inflamer

At low levels, Firmicutes are normal and harmless. But when they overgrow — usually from a high-fat, high-sugar diet — they produce chemicals that cause inflammation throughout the body. Athletes with too many Firmicutes report fatigue and slower recovery after games.

⚡ Thrives on: fatty foods, sugar, low-fiber diets
✅ Balanced by: diverse plant-based diet
The key idea: Good bacteria and harmful bacteria are always competing for space and food in your gut. What you eat tips the balance one way or the other — every single meal.

🎮

🔍 Round 2 Observations Answer Doc → R2

Which foods made the biggest difference to gut health? What do those foods have in common?
As a volleyball player, is a "healthy gut meal" realistic on a tournament day? What's one swap you could actually make?
The bacteria that benefit most from a healthy diet feed on fiber — the stuff in plants that your stomach can't break down but bacteria can. That's why fruits, vegetables, beans, and whole grains help the most. They're not just good for you, they're food for your gut team.
ROUND 3 · ~5 min

🍟 Player 3 — The Worst Meal

🎮
Player 3 drives this round. Build the meal that does the most damage to the gut team. Reset and go.
Challenge: Try to get the gut health score as low as possible. Can you get it below 20%?

🔍 Round 3 Observations Answer Doc → R3

Which bacteria went up on the worst meal? That bacteria going up is actually bad — why?
Look at Round 2 and Round 3 side by side. How different were the gut health scores? What does that tell you about food choices over time?
When good bacteria go down, the space they were taking up opens up for harmful bacteria — including ones that cause inflammation and make it harder to fight infections. This is why athletes who eat poorly sometimes get sick more often, even when they're in great physical shape.
