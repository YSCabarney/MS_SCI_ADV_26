The Great Organelle Election

Every organelle in the cell thinks it deserves the top spot. Your job between now and Thursday: pick ONE organelle, learn everything about it, and make a campaign poster arguing that it is the most important, most interesting, and most essential organelle in the entire cell.

On Thursday, you'll present your case — and the class will debate.

Step 1 — Choose Your Organelle

Each of you should pick a different organelle. Click the one you want to claim!

Think about which one excites you most — which one do you feel you can argue FOR?

Step 2 — Do Your Research 🔍

Before you make your poster, you need to know your organelle really well. Research these questions:

  • What does it look like? (Shape, size, what it looks like under a microscope)
  • What is its job? (Be specific — "it makes energy" isn't enough. What kind? How?)
  • Where is it found? (Plant cells? Animal cells? Both?)
  • How does its shape help it do its job? (structure-function!)
  • What would happen if it was missing? (This is your biggest argument!)
  • Any cool, surprising facts? (These win debates!)

🤖 About Using AI

You can use AI to help you find and understand information — that's totally fine! But here's the rule: your poster and arguments must be in your own words, with your own drawings. You can't just print or copy an AI response. The debate questions on Thursday will go beyond any facts AI gives you — you'll need to actually understand your organelle to answer them.

Step 3 — Design Your Campaign Poster 🎨

Your poster is your organelle's campaign ad. It needs to convince your classmates that YOUR organelle deserves the title of Best Organelle in the Cell.

Your poster MUST include:

  • 1

    A catchy campaign name or slogan

    Something memorable! This is your opening argument. Examples below 👇

  • 2

    A hand-drawn illustration of your organelle

    Draw it! Label the important parts. Make it look like a campaign logo or mascot. This should be the biggest part of your poster.

  • 3

    3 reasons why your organelle is the best

    Use science facts — structure, function, what would happen without it, real-world importance.

  • 4

    One "what would happen without me?" argument

    What would go wrong in the cell if your organelle disappeared? Make it dramatic. Make it true.

  • 5

    A real-world connection

    Connect your organelle to something in real life — a disease, a sport, a food, an everyday object. Anything that makes the judges (your classmates) care!

Slogan inspiration:

"Mitochondria: No Power? No Life." "Vote Nucleus: Because Someone Has to Be in Charge." "Ribosome: Building the Future, One Protein at a Time." "Golgi: On Time, Every Time." "Chloroplast: We Started the Food Chain."

📐 Poster Layout Ideas

Here are some ways you could organize your poster:

Campaign Ad Style
Big drawing + bold slogan at the top. Bullet points of reasons below. Like a real political campaign poster.
Magazine Cover Style
Your organelle as the "cover story." Headline: "The Most Important Organelle You Never Appreciated." Article preview bullets.
Sports Recruiting Poster
Like a player profile card — stats, skills, why this organelle should be the MVP of the cell.
Infographic Style
Use arrows, icons, and labels to show everything about the organelle. Makes it look professional and science-y.

⚔️ What Happens on Thursday: The Debate

Each person gets 2 minutes to present their poster and make their case.
After all three presentations, the group can challenge each other. Expect questions like: "Why is YOUR organelle better than hers?" or "Could the cell survive without yours?"
You'll need to defend your organelle — so really understand it, not just memorize facts.
There may be a vote at the end. 🏆 Come prepared to win.

🎨 What to Use

Use whatever art supplies you have! The more creative and colorful, the better.

  • Poster board, large paper, or even thick cardstock
  • Markers, colored pencils, watercolors, paint
  • Ruler for clean lines and labels
  • Your word wall cards + study guide for reference

You can print out a reference photo of your organelle to look at while you draw — just don't tape the printed picture onto your poster. The illustration should be yours!
