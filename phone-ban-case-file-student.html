Do Phone Bans Actually Work?

Schools everywhere are banning phones this year — maybe yours too. But does banning them actually make school better? Today, you're the investigators who decide.

10 minwith your team
3investigators, 3 jobs
No materialsneeded

The big question

A real debate happening in schools right now

As of 2026, most U.S. states now require schools to restrict cell phones during the day — some for the whole day, "bell to bell." Supporters say it will help students focus, feel better, and get along more. But here's the twist: real researchers who've actually studied it are split. One major 2025 study of over 1,200 students found no real difference in mental health, grades, or well-being between schools with strict bans and schools without one.

So which is it? Your team is about to investigate a mini version of this exact question — and uncover why it's a much trickier thing to test than it sounds.

The case

Two schools, one big question

Mountain View Middle School banned phones completely this year — bell to bell, no exceptions. Lakeside Middle School, across town, still lets students use phones at lunch. At the end of the year, researchers compare the two schools' average test scores and student happiness surveys to see which school did better.

Before you jump to an answer, remember that real scientists don't just guess and move on — they follow a process to make sure their thinking actually holds up. You and your team are going to work through that same process for this exact case, one step at a time, with your teacher guiding you along the way.

Read the case together with your team, then each investigator works their job below.

Your roles

Three investigators, three jobs

Investigator 1

The Change Spotter

Your job: figure out the ONE big thing that's actually different between what Mountain View and Lakeside are doing.

Report outWhat is the one thing being compared between the two schools?

Investigator 2

The Data Detective

Your job: figure out what's actually being measured to decide which school "did better."

Report outWhat two pieces of data are the researchers collecting? Which one do you think is easier to measure fairly, and why?

Investigator 3

The Fairness Checker

Your job: think about everything else that's different between two entirely different schools — not just the phone policy.

Report outName at least two other differences between Mountain View and Lakeside that have nothing to do with phones, but could still affect test scores or happiness.

Before you wrap up

One more question for your team

Mountain View and Lakeside aren't identical schools with one single difference — they're two completely different places. Talk it over with your team: if Mountain View ends up with higher test scores or happier students, can you be sure it's really because of the phone ban? Why or why not?
